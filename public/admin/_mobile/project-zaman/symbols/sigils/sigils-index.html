Sigils Index

Sigils Index

Tags: project-zaman symbols symbol sigil working

Purpose of this folder

One note per emblem, mark, or insignia. Each sigil should record what it depicts, who uses it, what it claims, and what it conceals — the gap between the last two is where this project’s symbolism works.

Established Sigils

The White Serpent — Gibborim

Defined in Gibborim. An open, hissing mouth and a tail shaped into an anchor-like hook.

ElementMeaning
White coloringPurity, revelation, divine authority — while concealing the violence beneath the claim
Open, hissing mouthDoctrine spoken as command
Anchor-hook tailPossession. What crosses into reach is secured, restrained, and drawn inward

It announces its authority. It does not merely observe knowledge; it claims it.

The Black Serpent — Axios

Defined in Axios. Depicted without hook, fang, crown, or ornament.

ElementMeaning
Black coloringObservation without declared intent
No hookNo claim of possession
Closed mouth, no fangSilence, precision, continuity

It does not strike. It watches.

Anthropos — No Public Symbol

Anthropos displays nothing. Worth treating as a deliberate design statement rather than an omission: the layer that touches the public is the layer that leaves no mark. A charity does not need a sigil; it needs a letterhead.

Other Marks Already in the Vault

  • Sigil markings on shimmering skin — attributed to the post-fission entity called The Instrument in Project Zaman. The only instance of a sigil appearing on a body rather than an object. Undefined.
  • The letter crate markings — the cult’s markings on the ornate crate that paid for a sold child. Alyna recognizes them instantly. The only time a sigil functions as evidence in the story.
  • Faction insignia — listed in The Oasis as one of the personal possessions an NPC will refuse to surrender.

Working Oasis Successor Marks

These symbols are proposed from existing faction ideology.

FactionMarkMeaning
The Order of the OasisUpright water drop inside a square frameRefuge preserved through discipline, boundary, and control.
The MirageBroken or wavering water dropThe original refuge seen as illusion, memory, or distorted promise.

Both should remain visibly descended from the original Oasis mark. Their difference should feel like an argument over the same inheritance.

Open Questions

  • What was the original Oasis mark? The child faction marks imply a shared water-drop parent symbol, but the exact original emblem still needs final design.
  • Did the original Oasis have a sigil at all? If it did, who kept it after the schism is a whole scene by itself.
  • What are the sigil markings on the shimmering skin? Gibborim’s script, Axios’ classification, or something neither faction authored?
  • Is there a mark for the MC? He exists outside the Hebrew naming system. Whether that means he is unmarked, or marked in a way the system cannot read, is a strong open question tied to Open Gaps #01.
  • How does the anchor-hook read at small scale? It has to survive being stamped on a crate, embroidered on a collar, and carved into a wall.

Note Template

Consistency rule already in place

The two cult serpents are built as an opposed pair: one speaks and seizes, the other watches and records. Any third cult sigil should extend that logic rather than introduce a new visual language.
