Purpose of this folder

What Project ZAMAN is drawing from, and — more usefully — what it is not taking. An influence note that only lists what you liked will quietly turn the project into an imitation. Recording the boundary is the point.

Declared Influences

Lies of P

Named in Project Zaman as an aesthetic reference, and again in The Oasis: the survivor faction structure was partly inspired by the Sweepers and Bastards, collectively associated with the Stalkers.

Taken: the period-industrial aesthetic; the idea of scattered factions of survivors with opposed philosophies whom the player can side with, betray, or reconcile.

Not taken: the puppet/humanity framework. Project ZAMAN’s equivalent question is about a soul divided by contradiction, not a construct earning humanity.

Bloodborne

Named in Project Zaman as an aesthetic reference.

Taken: the aesthetic register — early-modern setting with a metaphysical horror beneath it, and institutions that were respectable before they were monstrous.

Not taken, and worth stating clearly: the cosmic-indifference stance. Project ZAMAN’s catastrophe is not caused by an inhuman outside force. It is caused by one man’s refusal to lose, on top of a century of institutional ambition. The horror here is entirely human, and Themes is explicit that the ending is adaptation rather than transcendence.

The Genre Tension Already Identified

Themes states the position directly: where many Soulslikes hand the player a broken world and ask them to restore an earlier order — lighting the fire, ending the cycle, reclaiming the throne — Project ZAMAN refuses that structure. There is no earlier order worth restoring.

That is a deliberate break from the genre it belongs to, and it is the most important influence note in the project: it defines the shape by what it declines to do.

Worth Documenting

  • Non-game influences. Nothing is recorded yet. The project’s real subject matter — institutional exploitation dressed as charity, identity erased by classification, grief that cannot be resolved — has more precedent in literature and history than in games.
  • The Masonic framing. Real fraternal-order structure is doing a lot of quiet work in the cult’s Anthropos/Gibborim/Axios split. Worth noting what was borrowed and what was invented.
  • The naming framework’s sources. Hebrew and Greek are used with specific intent in Gibborim and Anthropos. The Arabic register is declared and still empty.

Note Format

The test to apply

For every influence, be able to finish the sentence: “This is like X, except —”. If the exception is hard to name, the influence is being copied rather than used.
