The Order of the Oasis

The Order of the Oasis

Tags: project-zaman factions faction child-faction order-of-the-oasis survivors protection denial active


“The Oath Preserved”

They kept the promise. They also narrowed the meaning of who deserved to be protected.

Parent: The Oasis
Common shortening: OOTO
Role: Protective successor faction
Primary territory: Settlements, supply routes, defensive outposts, and resource-gathering areas
Opposing child faction: The Mirage

Overview

The Order of the Oasis consists of those who remained loyal to the original foundation and its oath.

They were the first group to formally separate from the collapsing Oasis. Believing the parent faction had become ideologically impure, they claimed the founder’s principles and carried them into a new organization.

The Order believes humanity cannot afford to waste its remaining strength searching for uncertain answers. Its duty is to protect settlements, secure resources, defend vulnerable territory, and preserve what little stability remains.

To the Order, abandoning the original purpose of The Oasis would betray everything the founder built.

That conviction also makes the faction rigid. Its members often treat their interpretation of the founder’s oath as the only legitimate one, and may view former companions as corruptors rather than people responding differently to the same catastrophe.

Meaning of the Name

The word Order represents discipline, preservation, and ideological purity.

The faction does not consider itself a replacement for The Oasis. Its members see themselves as the rightful continuation of the parent faction after the original organization lost its way.

To its enemies, the name represents self-appointed authority and an attempt to monopolize the founder’s legacy.

Central Weakness: Denial

Despite claiming to protect humanity, the Order increasingly defines humanity only through the survivors and territories it can still see.

A vast portion of the human population remains unaccounted for after the Fabrication Demise. Their deaths have never been confirmed, yet the Order behaves as though nothing meaningful can be done for them. It avoids the possibility that some may still exist in altered, displaced, trapped, or otherwise unreachable states.

This creates a moral contradiction. The Order presents itself as humanity’s rightful protector while refusing to seriously investigate the fate of countless people simply because their condition cannot be verified.

This failure does not make the Order irrational. The Mirage has spent years exploring dangerous regions without producing a reliable method of rescue, reversal, or recovery. From the Order’s perspective, sacrificing resources needed by confirmed survivors for an unproven possibility would be another form of abandonment.

The Order’s failure is therefore not that it protects those who remain. Its failure is that protection has slowly become an excuse to stop asking who else might still be saved.

Common Locations

Members of the Order are usually encountered near:

  • Settlement regions.
  • Supply routes.
  • Defensive outposts.
  • Resource-gathering areas.
  • Former Anthropos bases.

Note

The game does not contain ordinary civilian populations. Settlement regions function as protected territories, faction outposts, or remnants of organized human activity.

General Behavior

Order members are often:

  • Disciplined.
  • Loyal.
  • Determined.
  • Protective of their territory.
  • Distrustful of those who question the original oath.
  • Convinced that compromise will weaken humanity further.
  • Reluctant to investigate populations whose survival cannot be confirmed.
  • Prone to disguising denial as practical restraint.

They are more commonly found in organized groups and tend to behave more cautiously than isolated Mirage members.

The design should show care, order, and pressure. Nothing should feel ornamental for its own sake.

Relationship to The Mirage

The Order views The Mirage as reckless, disloyal, and wasteful.

Its members believe the Mirage abandoned confirmed survivors in order to chase answers that may never exist. They can point to years of Mirage expeditions that produced relics, fragments, and stories, but no dependable salvation.

Even so, the Order needs what the Mirage carries: forbidden-site access, field records, and the courage to enter regions the Order refuses to study.

That dependence is the heart of the reunification path in The Oasis.
