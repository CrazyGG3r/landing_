The Mirage

The Mirage

Tags: project-zaman factions faction child-faction mirage survivors knowledge chaos active


“The Refuge That Vanished”

If The Oasis is gone, then perhaps it was always a mirage.

Parent: The Oasis
Role: Exploratory successor faction
Primary territory: Cult facilities, abandoned laboratories, ritual sites, and temporally distorted regions
Opposing child faction: The Order of the Oasis

Overview

The Mirage consists mainly of those who remained after The Order of the Oasis separated from the original faction.

They had already begun rejecting the old oath. To them, simply protecting what remained of humanity would only delay humanity’s eventual destruction.

The Order’s departure deepened their resentment. They believed the loyalists had abandoned them while claiming moral superiority and sole ownership over the founder’s legacy.

Rather than continue using a name they believed had become hollow, they eventually called themselves The Mirage.

Meaning of the Name

The name The Mirage began as a bitter judgment of The Oasis.

A mirage resembles safety from a distance, but offers nothing when finally reached. Mirage members believe The Oasis promised salvation while only preserving humanity long enough for it to die more slowly.

They do not necessarily consider their own faction false. Instead, they carry the name as an admission that the old refuge was never enough.

Their central belief can be expressed as:

If The Oasis is gone, then perhaps it was always a mirage.

Ideology

The Mirage believes knowledge is the only meaningful path toward salvation.

Its members abandoned the faction’s traditional territories and began travelling toward locations connected to the cult responsible for the Catastrophe.

Their primary objective is to discover:

  • The origin of the Catastrophe.
  • The purpose of the cult’s experiments.
  • The truth behind the Oscillation of Eras.
  • A possible method of overcoming its effects.

To the Order, this pursuit is reckless and wasteful.

To The Mirage, refusing to search for answers is only a slower form of surrender.

Central Weakness: Chaos

The Mirage has the courage to enter places others avoid, but not the knowledge or discipline required to understand everything it finds.

Most of its members are capable fighters, gifted writers, or both. They can survive hostile expeditions, recover fragments of evidence, and record what they witness with care.

However, these abilities do not automatically make them investigators.

The faction lacks a consistent research method, shared analytical discipline, and enough understanding of the cult’s machinery, rituals, metaphysics, and experiments. Its members often return with damaged records, unexplained objects, contradictory accounts, and discoveries they cannot interpret.

As a result, The Mirage often mistakes movement for progress.

Its exploration is not meaningless. Its fighters keep expeditions alive, while its writers preserve details that might otherwise vanish. The tragedy is that the faction continues collecting evidence without the framework needed to turn that evidence into something useful for humanity.

The Mirage may be searching in the correct places, but in its current state, it does not know how to recognize the answer even if it finds one.

Common Locations

Members of The Mirage are usually encountered near:

  • Cult facilities.
  • Abandoned laboratories.
  • Ritual sites.
  • Temporally distorted regions.
  • Areas under strong cult influence.

General Behavior

Mirage members are often:

  • Independent.
  • Rebellious.
  • Sharp-witted.
  • Suspicious of authority.
  • Resentful of the Order’s claim to ideological purity.
  • Willing to break old laws in pursuit of the truth.
  • Usually skilled in combat, writing, or both.
  • Poorly equipped to analyze many of their own discoveries.
  • Prone to confusing exploration with meaningful progress.

Many members operate alone, although smaller exploration groups may also be encountered.

Working Visual Identity

The Mirage should look like a faction that crossed the map too many times and kept writing anyway.

ElementDirection
PaletteSun-faded blue, ash gray, weathered black, and pale cloth.
InsigniaA broken or wavering water-drop mark, as if the original Oasis symbol has become a heat-shimmer reflection.
SilhouetteAsymmetrical travel gear, notebooks or scroll cases, wrapped limbs, field tools, and improvised protection.
Material languageDust-stained fabric, cracked leather, handwritten tags, salvaged cult fragments, and repaired weapons.

The design should show courage, disorder, and accumulated evidence. They should look capable in the field, but never fully uniform.

Relationship to the Order

The Mirage views The Order of the Oasis as fearful, self-righteous, and morally incomplete.

Its members believe the Order abandoned the unknown victims of the Fabrication Demise by deciding only confirmed survivors were worth protecting. They can point to the Order’s refusal to investigate the unconfirmed population as proof that the old oath has narrowed into denial.

Even so, the Mirage needs what the Order preserves: discipline, supplies, safe territory, and a structure capable of protecting people during a long investigation.

That dependence is the heart of the reunification path in The Oasis.
