Tags: project-zaman production production design open

Purpose of this folder

How Project ZAMAN plays. Everything here is implementation-facing; the fiction it draws on lives in Lore/ and World/.

Established

Genre3D Action / Adventure · Soulslike
EngineUndecided · New Input System
Aesthetic referencesLies of P, Bloodborne
Player characterSubject-001 — the soul-dominant fragment
CiviliansNone. Settlement regions are protected territories, faction outposts, or remnants of organized activity
Animation directionAnimation Mechanics - contextual dodge and parry variants based on incoming attacks

Source: Project Zaman, Subject-001, The Oasis.

The One System That Is Actually Designed

The Oasis contains a complete faction system — seven player paths with consequences, NPC behavior split by solo versus grouped, a two-stage looting system where the second attempt provokes fury because the remaining item is personal, and a “best” outcome that requires the player to prove both factions hold incomplete halves of the same answer.

It is the most production-ready document in the vault. Everything below is comparatively undefined.

Systems Not Yet Designed

Combat. The full system is still open. Animation Mechanics now establishes one concrete direction: variant dodge and parry animations that respond to the opponent’s attack. Stamina, posture or poise, weapon movesets, and the checkpoint-and-recovery loop still need design.

The miracles as mechanics. MC establishes that all three require internal calm and degrade under fear, grief, anger, and panic. Open Gaps #02 settles that Subject-001 does not directly inherit them, while their broader post-fission fate remains unresolved. If any miracle becomes playable, the design must explain how it returns, echoes, or operates through a changed form.

The shadow form. Open Gaps #07 asks whether it can be resisted, accelerated, or redirected through player choice. A risk-reward transformation is the obvious reading, but nothing is decided.

The Desert and the Hope Entity. The Hope Entity specifies conditions that bring the entity nearer or push it further, tied to Subject-001’s coherence rather than his mood. That is a legible progression system in all but name. Open Gaps #04 asks whether reconnection is one event or a gradual process, and whether the player can fail or delay it.

Subject-002 encounters. Established as only ever seen at a distance. Whether that is a scripted sighting, a wandering presence, or something the player can pursue is unresolved — as is their eventual relationship, Open Gaps #03.

Death and recovery. The Irreversibility Principle says nothing is restored. A genre built on repeated death and retrieval sits in direct tension with that. Worth resolving deliberately rather than by default.

Open Questions

  • What is the core combat loop, and what does the player actually hold?
  • Do the miracles become abilities, or stay narrative?
  • What is the cost of using one? Nothing in the vault describes fatigue or backlash.
  • How does the game express calm as a mechanical state the player manages?
  • What does dying mean in a world where nothing is restored?
  • Is the Desert a playable space, a cutscene, or a recurring interstitial?
  • Scope: how many locations, bosses, and hours is this actually aiming at?

The design question worth answering first

This game’s abilities are switched off by fear, anger, and panic. Its genre is built on producing exactly those states in the player. Deciding whether that is a contradiction to solve or the entire point will determine most of the systems above.
