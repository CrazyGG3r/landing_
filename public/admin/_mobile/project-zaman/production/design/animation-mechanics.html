Animation Mechanics

Animation Mechanics

Tags: project-zaman production production design animation dodge parry proposed

Purpose

This note defines a combat-animation direction for Project ZAMAN: contextual defensive animations that react to the opponent’s attack instead of using one generic dodge or parry response.

Design Goal

Project ZAMAN’s combat should feel grounded, readable, and tense.

Defensive animation can support that by making the player character respond to the specific threat in front of him. A horizontal sweep, a direct thrust, an overhead strike, and a low attack should not all trigger the same body motion.

The goal is realism through response quality, not animation excess.

Variant Dodge Mechanics

Variant Dodge Mechanics activate dedicated dodge animations based on the opponent’s incoming attack.

The system can read several attack properties:

  • Attack height: high, mid, or low.
  • Attack line: horizontal, vertical, thrusting, circular, or diagonal.
  • Attack source: weapon, limb, projectile, grab, or environmental hazard.
  • Player direction: forward, backward, left, right, or angled.
  • Timing quality: early, clean, late, or panic input.
  • Distance: close pressure, normal range, or barely-in-range evasion.

This allows defensive movement to look intentional:

Incoming attackPossible dodge response
Horizontal sweepDuck, shoulder roll, or compact side slip
Straight thrustNarrow sidestep or angled pivot
Overhead strikeBack step, shoulder turn, or diagonal escape
Low sweepHop, lifted step, or backward pull
Grab attemptHand break, torso twist, or short shove-away
ProjectileLean, drop, side roll, or cover-aware evade

The animation should never hide the player’s intention. Even when a contextual variant plays, the input direction and timing still need to be clear.

Variant Parry Mechanics

Variant Parry Mechanics use dedicated parry reactions based on the incoming attack’s angle, force, and source.

A strong parry system should not make every successful defense look like the same weapon tap. It should show how Subject-001 survives pressure while still appearing physically vulnerable.

Possible variants:

Incoming attackPossible parry response
Light weapon strikeQuick deflection with minimal recovery
Heavy weapon strikeTwo-handed brace, recoil, or staggered redirect
ThrustOutside-line deflection or wrist turn
Overhead strikeRaised guard, angled catch, or shoulder drop
Claw or limb attackForearm jam, body slip, or elbow check
Repeated combo hitChain parry with rising stamina or posture cost

The parry should reward accurate timing while still respecting the fiction. A perfect response can look skillful, but not effortless.

Readability Rules

  • Do not let contextual animations steal control for too long.
  • Keep dodge and parry outcomes readable at boss speed.
  • Use clear anticipation frames on enemy attacks so the player can learn patterns.
  • Match animation intensity to risk: a late dodge should look less composed than a clean one.
  • Preserve the Soulslike demand for commitment, stamina, spacing, and recovery.
  • Avoid making defensive variety purely cosmetic; the chosen variant should communicate why it worked.

Lore Fit

This system suits Subject-001 because his survival is not based on brute confidence.

He carries inherited grief, fragmented identity, and a body moving through danger without full certainty. Contextual dodge and parry animations can express that: not invincible grace, but precise survival under pressure.

The system should also respect the broader premise of Immaculate Thought and the calm requirement recorded in Mechanics Index. If defensive mastery ever connects to calm, it should feel like controlled presence rather than aggression.
