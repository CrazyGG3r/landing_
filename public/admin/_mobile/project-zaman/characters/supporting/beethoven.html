Beethoven

Beethoven

Tags: project-zaman characters character supporting-character boss beethoven music temptation partial

”The symphony that helps the player survive may eventually turn against him.”

Role: Recurring boss assistant and possible opponent
Condition: Blind
Mentor: Mozart
Desire: A Time Shard capable of reclaiming what his ambition cost him

Concept

Beethoven is Project ZAMAN’s reimagining of the historical musician as a blind symphony fighter. This is an alternate-fiction interpretation within the fractured setting, not a claim about the historical person.

He sacrificed his sight in pursuit of fame and musical passion despite the warning of his mentor, Mozart. What began as devotion to art gradually curdled into greed. In the playable era, that desire draws him toward a Time Shard that might restore his sight or retrieve a reality in which he never lost it.

Beethoven can help Subject-001 without possessing pure motives. His assistance, ambition, regret, and eventual betrayal should remain present at the same time.

Symphony Fighter

Beethoven fights through music. The exact weapon, physical choreography, and metaphysical rules remain open, but his combat identity should make rhythm, phrasing, silence, and escalating musical structure legible during battle.

His blindness should shape his fighting method rather than function as decorative tragedy. Sound, vibration, memory, timing, and spatial rhythm may allow him to read an arena differently from sighted fighters.

Boss Assistance

Before certain boss encounters, the player can collect musical notes across repeated attempts. Filling the required quota allows Beethoven to enter the arena and assist Subject-001.

This system rewards observation, persistence, and knowledge of the encounter rather than raw combat skill alone. His arrival should feel like the completion of a musical phrase built across previous attempts.

Across these assists, subtle irregularities should foreshadow his later betrayal:

  • A wrong or dissonant note at an important moment.
  • A pause suggesting that he is listening for something beyond the battle.
  • Unusual interest in Time Shards or their locations.
  • A choice that protects his objective before protecting Subject-001.
  • References to Mozart that omit, distort, or resent the mentor’s role.

The clues should become meaningful in retrospect without making the betrayal obvious in advance.

Betrayal of Mozart

Mozart was Beethoven’s true mentor and opposed the sacrifice of his sight. Beethoven later betrayed him. The exact act, timing, and consequences of that betrayal remain undefined.

This history should be felt throughout Beethoven’s assistance sequences before it is explained. His treatment of Mozart reveals the pattern he may repeat with Subject-001: accepting guidance while resenting any person who stands between him and what he desires.

Turning Against Subject-001

In the proposed True Secret Ending, Beethoven turns against Subject-001 after the player has invested significant effort in collecting notes and building the relationship.

The confrontation must feel earned. His assistance remains real, but so does his desire for the shard. The boss fight should force the player to confront the person they learned to rely upon rather than reveal that every earlier interaction was meaningless.

His final role, survival, possible mercy, and exact demand remain open.
