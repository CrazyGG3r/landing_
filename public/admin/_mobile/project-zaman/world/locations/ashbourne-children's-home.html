Ashbourne Children's Home

Ashbourne Children’s Home

Tags: project-zaman world location ashbourne anthropos established

“A charity with a locked second purpose.”

Type: Orphanage; Anthropos acquisition and observation site
Era: Before the Oscillation of Eras
Controlled by: Anthropos
Public Status: Charitable children’s home
True Status: Burned, evacuated, and later revisited by the rescued children
Post-Oscillation Status: Unresolved

Overview

Ashbourne Children’s Home presents itself as an ordinary charitable orphanage: a respectable institution where abandoned children are given shelter, education, and the possibility of adoption.

In truth, Ashbourne forms part of Anthropos’s hidden recruitment and observation network.

The cult uses the orphanage to identify children who display unusual cognitive, spiritual, or soul-based potential. Those considered valuable are documented, monitored, and eventually transferred into more controlled environments through adoption, sponsorship, medical referral, or other publicly acceptable means.

The original MC spends most of his childhood at Ashbourne. His life there changes when Noam reports the first two miracles, leading to the MC’s public adoption, private extraction, and eventual captivity.

Hidden Purpose

Ashbourne serves three purposes within the Anthropos system.

Acquisition

The orphanage gives Anthropos access to children who possess no stable family protection and whose disappearances can be concealed through forged adoption records, transfers, illness reports, or institutional relocation.

Observation

Children are monitored over long periods for unusual patterns in behavior, cognition, intuition, memory, emotional influence, and spiritual sensitivity.

Most are never told they are being studied.

Consequence Analysis

Ashbourne also functions as an early-warning environment.

Anthropos does not fully understand the forces it is attempting to control. Because of this, the cult uses the orphanage to observe what consequences may emerge around unusual children before exposing its primary laboratories, personnel, and research sites.

Minor incidents, behavioral changes, shared dreams, unexplained coincidences, miracles, and disturbances are documented as possible indicators of a larger metaphysical reaction.

Ashbourne therefore becomes both a testing ground and a buffer. If something goes wrong, the event can be blamed on poor management, childhood imagination, illness, fire, institutional neglect, or religious superstition.

The children bear the consequence. The true facilities remain hidden.

The Decoy Institution

Ashbourne is publicly visible by design.

Its existence draws attention away from Anthropos’s more important locations and provides the cult with a believable source for records concerning displaced children. Investigators searching for missing subjects are led toward the orphanage, its fabricated administrators, and its falsified adoption history rather than the facilities where the children are actually taken.

This makes Ashbourne a decoy source in two ways:

  • It provides a false administrative origin for children already moved into Anthropos custody.

  • It absorbs suspicion when an experiment, disappearance, or unexplained event threatens to become public.

The orphanage is expendable. Its records, staff identities, and legal ownership can all be abandoned once they become dangerous.

Narrative Function

Ashbourne is the story’s first institutional mask.

It appears to represent care, safety, and public charity. Beneath that surface, it is a sorting mechanism built around children the cult considers useful.

It establishes one of the central truths of Project ZAMAN:

Anthropos rarely hides evil behind darkness.
It hides it behind respectable systems.

Ashbourne is important because:

  • It is the original MC’s childhood home.

  • It is the first place where Noam’s assignment becomes morally dangerous.

  • It introduces Anthropos’s habit of disguising control as protection.

  • It allows the cult to study possible consequences without exposing its primary facilities.

  • It becomes the site where Alyna’s resistance and the MC’s third miracle quietly operate in parallel.

  • Its destruction turns an expendable decoy into a source of evidence the cult can no longer fully contain.

The Ashbourne Fire

The fire does not begin as an attempt to destroy the entire orphanage.

It starts inside a targeted compartment containing something Anthropos considers too dangerous to preserve. The intended destruction is controlled, limited, and designed to appear accidental.

The fire spreads beyond its original boundary.

Once the incident becomes visible to the public, Anthropos is forced to distance itself from the institution. Staff records collapse into invented identities, ownership trails lead nowhere, and the orphanage is temporarily abandoned.

For three days, the building remains burned, unsecured, and unrepaired.

During this period, the rescued children return to retrieve their belongings. Without understanding their importance, they carry away damaged papers, labelled objects, fragments of records, and other materials from the building.

What Anthropos intended to erase is instead dispersed.

What Is Known

  • Ashbourne operates as a legitimate public orphanage.

  • Its true ownership is concealed behind shell organisations, false records, and invented names.

  • The institution is connected to Anthropos’s recruitment network.

  • Children are observed for unusual cognitive, spiritual, and soul-based traits.

  • Noam is placed inside Ashbourne as an observer.

  • The original MC is selected after the first two miracles are reported.

  • Anthropos fails to understand the third miracle before extracting him.

  • The orphanage acts as a decoy administrative source for children transferred into cult custody.

  • It is also used to study possible consequences before similar risks are introduced into primary facilities.

  • The fire begins inside a targeted compartment but spreads into a larger public disaster.

  • The building remains empty for three days following the fire.

  • The children later return and unknowingly scatter evidence beyond the cult’s immediate reach.

Thematic Meaning

Ashbourne represents protection without safety.

The children are fed, housed, and educated, yet every act of care is compromised by the institution’s hidden purpose. They are not protected because their lives are valued. They are preserved because they may become useful.

The orphanage also reflects Anthropos’s fear of consequences.

The cult presents itself as confident and enlightened, but Ashbourne reveals its uncertainty. Anthropos is willing to experiment with forces it does not understand, provided someone else stands close enough to suffer first.

Open Questions

  • Does Ashbourne still exist after the Oscillation of Eras?

  • Can the player visit its remains during the playable present?

  • Was the targeted compartment an archive, dormitory, hidden laboratory, observation room, or something else?

  • What evidence did the children remove from the building?

  • Did any surviving records eventually reach journalists, authorities, or rival organisations?

  • Was the spreading fire genuinely accidental, or was it influenced by the MC’s third miracle?

  • Did Anthropos ever restore Ashbourne’s public reputation?

  • Was Ashbourne the only institution of its kind?

  • Did the cult abandon the orphanage after the fire, or continue using it under another name?

  • What specific consequence was Anthropos afraid might occur there?
