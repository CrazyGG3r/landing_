Setting Overview

Setting Overview

Tags: project-zaman world index setting working

Purpose of this folder

The physical and social world: what kind of place this is, what technology exists, who holds power, and what daily life looks like on both sides of the Catastrophe. Specific places go in World/Locations; the order of events goes in World/Era & Timeline.

Established

Period. An early 20th century world, fractured by the misuse of the soul (Project Zaman).

Two states of the world. The setting exists in two distinct conditions, and almost every note in the vault belongs to one or the other:

Before the OscillationAfter the Oscillation
RealityIntactPermanently desynchronized; historical eras collide in the same space
The facilityOperationalA decayed, distorted structure suspended in temporal disarray
The cultAnthropos public, Gibborim hidden, Axios recordingAll on-site scientists and personnel killed
PopulationOrdinary society, newspapers, harbors, inns, mansionsLargely unaccounted for after the Fabrication Demise
Organized humanityInstitutions, households, publishersFaction remnants: the Order of the Oasis and The Mirage

Social texture that already exists in the notes. Newspapers and media that can be steered by a respected family. Wealthy households that hire on reputation and discretion. A harbor with working women carrying loads. Rented flats with thin walls. Inns busy enough to disappear into. Secret publishers who can be tracked, shut down, and interrogated. Undercover troops passing as beggars, drunkards, and middle-class citizens. A charitable orphanage nobody thinks to question.

No ordinary civilians in play. The Oasis states the game contains no civilian population. Settlement regions function as protected territories, faction outposts, or remnants of organized human activity.

Power structure. Anthropos holds public legitimacy through charity, academic patronage, medical initiatives, and civic development. Gibborim directs it from behind separated chains of authority. Axios supplies the technical foundation for both. See Anthropos, Gibborim, Axios.

Open Questions

  • Where is this? No country, city, or region is named anywhere in the vault. Ashbourne, the harbor, and the mansion all exist without a map. Deciding whether to name the setting or keep it deliberately unplaced is a real choice with consequences for the naming framework.
  • How much technology? Weapons, wheelchairs, newspapers, and laboratory instruments appear. Whether there are cars, telephones, electric light, or trains has not been set, and it affects how Alyna’s underground network plausibly operates.
  • What does the collision of eras actually look like? “Different historical eras collide in the same space” is the single most distinctive feature of the post-Oscillation world and the least described. Which eras? Visible seams, or seamless? Can a player walk from one into another?
  • How long after the Oscillation does the playable story begin? Long enough for The Oasis to be founded, lose its founder, and split into two factions — but no duration is given.
  • Does anything outside the affected region survive? Whether the Oscillation is global or bounded determines whether “humanity” in The Oasis’s oath means everyone or only the survivors nearby.
  • Is the cult entirely gone? All on-site personnel died. Whether Gibborim survives elsewhere decides who the late-game antagonist actually is.

Suggested Notes for This Folder

  • The World Before the Oscillation — the intact society, for scenes set in the MC’s childhood and Alyna’s campaign
  • The World After the Oscillation — the playable present
  • Technology and Daily Life
  • Society and Class — Alyna’s uniform grants access precisely because of how this world sees servants
  • What Remains of Humanity — population, settlements, the Fabrication Demise

Anchor to protect

The horror of this setting is institutional rather than supernatural. An orphanage, a respected family, a research division, a newspaper. The metaphysics only work because the ordinary world around them is completely believable.
